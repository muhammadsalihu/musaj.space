## Script 2 — "Customizing a Hermes Agent to Your Workflow"
**Source article:** `content/customizing-a-hermes-agent-to-your-workflow.md`

> **[HOOK — first 5 seconds]** People ask my secret for getting AI agents to actually behave. There isn't one. There's a method. Here's the whole thing, in order.
>
> **[BODY]** Step zero — before you touch the agent, write down how you actually work. What's "done"? What do you keep re-typing? That list is your spec.
>
> One — shape the system prompt like a job description. Who are you, what are your rules, what's your output contract? Tight and imperative.
>
> Two — stop cramming everything into the prompt. Push recurring behavior into skills — small playbooks the agent loads when a task matches. That's the difference between an agent that's *reminded* how to work and one that *knows*.
>
> Three — give it durable memory. The good stuff only. Four — wire the right tools, least privilege. Five — here's the one nobody does — test it like code. Happy path, edge cases, regression. Agents drift. Re-run the tests or you're just hoping.
>
> **[CTA]** The magic isn't smarter AI — it's making your work legible enough to hand to a machine. If that sounds like your business, let's build it. Follow for the next one — I'm musaj.

**Word count:** ~140

## Script 3 — "Shipping Production Apps with AI Agents"
**Source article:** `content/shipping-production-apps-with-ai-agents.md`

> **[HOOK — first 5 seconds]** Nobody tells you a production AI agent is a brilliant, tireless, occasionally reckless junior engineer you can't fire. Here's how I ship with one anyway — and the failures that taught me.
>
> **[BODY]** The agent is not an oracle. It's a force multiplier. Treat it like a junior who needs guardrails, and your velocity goes up instead of turning into fire-fighting.
>
> The failures? It refactored an auth flow exactly as I asked — and blew a session edge case that only surfaced under real load. Code was valid. Tests passed. The instruction was subtly wrong and it didn't push back. That's the feature of delegation — a bad intent at full speed.
>
> So the rules: the agent owns the mechanical eighty percent, I own the decisions. Tests are the contract — I write the test as the spec before it writes code. Every production change gets a human diff review. Least privilege by default. And context — feed it well and it acts like a senior, starve it and it acts like an overconfident junior.
>
> **[CTA]** Shipping with agents isn't a hack. It's a real discipline. If you're shipping every day like me, follow — I'm musaj, and I build with agents that don't ship blind.

**Word count:** ~135
